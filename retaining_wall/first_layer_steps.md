# Base Course & Drainage Sequence — Lower Tier

Six steps, in order, with the actual measurements for your 24–30" wall.

Line the entirety of the hollowed out dirt rectangle. Look at the image in this folder for more clarification.

## 1. Excavate & Line the Trench

- **Width:** minimum 24" per the manual — that covers 6" of base material in front of the block, 12" for the block itself, and 6" behind it.
- **But extend it further back than that.** The 24" figure only leaves 6" behind the block, not the full 12" clean-stone drainage zone the manual requires. Dig to roughly **30" wide** (6" front + 12" block + 12" drainage zone) so the stone column gets its full width — more still if your geogrid design calls for extra length back into the bank.
- **Depth:** minimum 6" of compacted base material. For your wall height, the height-based embedment ratio (1" of buried block per 8–10" of total wall height) computes to under 6", so the **6" floor governs** — plan on burying 6" of the base block below finished grade.
- **Your math is right:** an 8" base block with 6" buried leaves about **2" showing above grade** at the toe once this course is in. Total dig depth at the front of the trench comes out to roughly **12" below finished grade** (6" base material + 6" buried block).
- Line the full excavation — bottom and both sides — with the trench liner fabric before anything else goes in. Leave extra material at the top edges on both sides to fold over later.

## 2. Leveling Pad

- Compact a minimum **6"** of road base to **95% Standard Proctor**, across the full ~30" width of the excavation — not just under the block footprint, but under where the drainage zone will sit too.
- Check level with a transit or hand level before setting any block. This surface determines whether everything above goes in straight.

## 3. Base Course

- Center each block on the pad with **6" of base material in front and 6" behind**.
- Level side-to-side and front-to-back, checking across three blocks at a time with a hand level; adjust with a rubber mallet.
- At your chosen daylight joint, leave a gap sized to your pipe's outside diameter — for a 4" perforated pipe plus its coupling, plan on roughly a **4.5"–5" gap**.

## 4. Fabric Curtain

- Since your finished wall is short, use **one continuous piece for the full wall height** rather than cutting it per course. Cut it to roughly **34"–40" tall**: your ~28" target wall height, plus 4"–6" extra at the bottom for the lip, plus another 4"–6" at the top to fold over the finished stone once the wall is capped.
- Stand it up on the **backfill side** of the future 12"-wide gravel zone — never between the gravel and the block.
- At the base, fold **4"–6" outward** into the backfill side, overlapping the trench liner underneath so there's no gap at that seam.
- Right now, only the bottom ~8" of this tall piece is doing anything (this course's height) — the rest stays loosely gathered above until later courses catch up to it.
- Hold it in place with a couple of fabric staples through the lip, letting it lean on the block, and backfilling in short sections right behind it as you go — the gravel weight is what actually locks it in.

## 5. Gravel Bedding

- Fill the 12"-wide zone (behind the block, inside the curtain) with **2"–3"** of 3/4" clean crushed stone as bedding before the pipe goes in.
- This sits directly on the same road base pad — nothing separates them — and directly against the block and open cores.

## 6. Drainage Pipe

- Typically a **4" perforated pipe** (with its own factory fabric sock), resting on the 2"–3" gravel bedding — never on the compacted pad directly, and never higher than roughly the bottom of the first course.
- Pitch it consistently the whole run. A common minimum is around **1% fall** (roughly 1/8" per foot), which works out to about **3"–4" of total fall** across your 30 ft wall from high end to the daylight gap.

---

**Next:** pack more 3/4" stone around and over the pipe, filling out the rest of this course's 8" height. Backfill with road base behind the curtain up to that same height. Since the curtain is one continuous piece, you don't fold anything over yet — that happens only once you reach the very top of the wall, just below the cap. Course 2 goes on next, and the same curtain simply continues upward as you keep filling.
